Case Study / Structural Addition

Engineering a seamless home addition in Scarsdale.

A 2,500 sq. ft. extension designed for luxury living in Westchester County — blending structural steel, open-plan flow, custom millwork, and architectural continuity with the original home.

Project Narrative

The challenge: architectural continuity.

In historic Lower Westchester neighborhoods such as Scarsdale and Bronxville, a large-scale home addition has to do more than create extra square footage. It has to respect the original architecture, preserve the home’s character, and still deliver modern function for daily life.

The client wanted a modern kitchen extension and primary suite addition that looked as though it had always belonged to the home. That required careful planning around structure, rooflines, exterior proportions, trim details, interior transitions, and the relationship between old and new spaces.

Structural steel made the open plan possible.

Creating a wide open-concept kitchen and dining connection required complex structural steel installation. By coordinating industrial-grade I-beams, we opened a 40-foot span without relying on intrusive vertical columns that would interrupt the finished living space.

This kind of work demands early coordination between framing, mechanical systems, electrical routing, ceiling planes, trim transitions, and finish sequencing. When the structural decisions are handled early, the finished space feels calm instead of compromised.

Field Note

The most expensive renovation mistakes often happen when structure, materials, utilities, and finish expectations are planned separately. The smoother approach is to coordinate those decisions before the build gains momentum.

Regional permitting across Westchester, Putnam, and Fairfield.

Navigating wetland permits in Mahopac, historic board reviews in Greenwich, CT, or zoning review in Westchester can add significant time when the process is not managed clearly. For this case study, municipal requirements were coordinated early to keep the project moving.

Whether the project involves a lakefront addition in Putnam County, a coastal renovation in Darien, or a structural remodel in Scarsdale, familiarity with regional building expectations helps reduce delays, avoid stop-work issues, and protect the project schedule.
